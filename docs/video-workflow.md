# From an idea to a finished video

Use this when a story needs video. A text-only post can skip media; start with the [beginner guide](quickstart.md). One person can do every role, with AI assisting where available. The creator owns the experience, permissions, editorial decisions and final acceptance.

## 1. Choose one real story

What problem did you encounter, what did you do, and what happened or what did you learn? Separate supported facts, your own observations and unknowns. Identify the audience question and what they can take away. Provide only sources you are allowed to share.

## 2. Find an opening

Offer a concrete result, contrast, question or lived moment that makes the story worth watching. Develop the title, cover wording and opening together. Their promise must match the supported story; an attractive hook is not a license to invent results.

## 3. Write the post and narration

Prepare a natural first-person script, description and relevant topic suggestions. Preserve the author's voice and actual angle. Check every claim against its source and remove invented feelings, times, results and unsupported claims. Prepare another language only if needed; keep the same supported facts. The author reviews the draft.

## 4. Map words to shots

Copy the [shot and filming checklist](../templates/video-shot-list.md). For each section, note what the audience should see, the actual source and anything to film or obscure. Make one compact pickup list. Add camera position and movement only where helpful; still shots are fine. Establish whether a shot proves a claim or simply illustrates it.

## 5. Collect footage

Prefer permitted real footage, screenshots and recordings for real-world claims. Verify dates, versions, rights, privacy and required consent. Mark renders, mockups and generated images as illustrative; do not pass them off as real events or proof. Record missing footage without inventing an outcome.

## 6. Produce narration

Use the creator's own recording or a specifically authorized voice. Keep the script, voice provenance and actual revision together. Listen to the entire result for pronunciation, natural pacing and missing words. This repository does not provide a voice or imply voice consent.

## 7. Make the video and cover

Edit the selected footage with the narration, readable captions, licensed audio and an appropriate cover. Preserve the source layers and revisions. Check language, framing and platform-safe areas. Generated media is optional: before a paid generation confirm the service, estimated cost, inputs and explicit authorization. Review text and reference images first; try one or two representative or difficult shots before expanding. Where supported and useful, compare start/end frames for continuity, without assuming they guarantee success. Preserve candidates; before a retry adjust the cause rather than repeating blindly.

## 8. Watch and listen from start to finish

Check the opening, factual claims, picture-to-speech alignment, subtitles, audio, rights, privacy and whether a phone viewer can understand the evidence. A reviewer unfamiliar with the project may perform a cold read of just the needed material, or an available AI may assist in a fresh context. This is optional support, not factual proof or a substitute for the creator watching and accepting the complete candidate; no specific tool or new thread is mandatory.

## 9. Prepare each chosen destination

For each chosen platform, prepare its title, description, relevant topic suggestions, language, cover, disclosures and real links. Verify current requirements. A draft, upload, scheduled post and public release are separate actions; follow the creator's actual authorization and reuse valid approval.

## 10. Publish, verify and archive

After an authorized release, reopen the management record and public surface where available. Record actual platform, version, identity, link, time and evidence; keep unavailable states unknown. Put each item's draft, shot list, source material, authorized audio, video and cover in a clear folder, separating history, candidates and accepted deliverables. Keep one current editing source; compare file count and hashes when copying to an archive, retain originals and do not claim automatic sync from a one-time copy.

## 11. Learn for the next one

Note what opening or footage worked, what needed rework and why, and one improvement to try. Record observations separately from guesses; measure only after an actual published identity exists. A few sentences are enough.
