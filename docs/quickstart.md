# Make your first content draft

The first session ends with a usable **draft and a precise missing-assets list**. Filming, publishing and results come later when their requirements are met. No coding or platform login is needed for this session.

## Prepare one small source folder

Choose something you actually did and can show: a repair, a tool, a before/after result or a failed attempt with a useful lesson. Select only the notes and media needed for this story. Do not give an assistant access to an entire personal drive or conversation archive.

Use any text editor. If you use a cloud AI service, send only material you are allowed to share with that service. Keep private originals separately and provide redacted extracts where sufficient.

Copy [the worksheet](../templates/content-worksheet.md) to your existing private content location. If you have no content system yet, use a folder containing the worksheet and its explicitly referenced assets. When you start tracking multiple items, choose one index; do not keep independent status copies in every app.

## Prepare the first draft

Fill worksheet sections 1–4 for this first session. Set the editorial status to `drafted` when a draft exists; keep destination receipts and metrics in sections 5–6 empty until those events occur. The steps below are actions, not worksheet section numbers.

1. **Reader and result:** finish “This helps ___ understand/do ___.” Select one question. An internal progress update alone is not a reader benefit.
2. **Author angle:** write two or three sentences in your own words about why you care. Include only experiences you actually had. This is the raw material for voice, not a request to polish everything yet.
3. **Claims:** list what happened, the supporting file and its date/version. Distinguish observations from conclusions. Put uncertainties in the unknown column.
4. **Story:** use the prompt to draft one title, short cover text and a spoken opening, then the body and ending. They should promise the same result.
5. **Review:** read it aloud. Mark language you would not say. Check every new experience, number, time estimate and performance claim against the source.
6. **Shots:** assign a real asset to each claim needing visual proof. For missing footage, write the action to film, framing and what the clip must demonstrate.

An assistant may suggest alternate phrasing; it may not fill gaps with invented frustration, conversations, reactions, failures or motives. If the opening promises a result that the media cannot show, revise it or wait for the shot.

## Save a handoff that survives a new chat

Save the selected text and missing-shots list in the same worksheet. Give the revision a label, point to that revision from your existing index, and mark earlier drafts as superseded without deleting source evidence. Record the next action and who owns it, such as “author to provide current touch-response footage.”

Pass the worksheet to the next helper with a narrow request: “Read this revision and these three source files; prepare the shot plan; do not rewrite approved claims.” Do not require it to reconstruct decisions from a long chat.

## Your first-session check

- Can another person explain the reader question and your point of view?
- Do the title, cover and first sentence tell the same story?
- Can each factual claim be traced to a source with a time/version?
- Are proposed shots distinguished from footage you already have?
- Is the current draft and its next action obvious?

If yes, continue with [the complete workflow](creator-workflow.md). If not, fix the specific gap; generating more variants usually will not fix missing evidence or an unclear angle.
