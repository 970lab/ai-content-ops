# Preserve the author's voice

## Establish the angle before asking for polish

Capture the author's own answer to three questions: What surprised you? What do you want the reader to see? What can they take away? Keep their distinctive phrases where accurate. A small everyday achievement may be the story even when the underlying technical log is long.

Draft title, cover and opening as one unit. The title creates interest, the cover is readable at small size, and the opening quickly delivers the same promise. Put exact model/version information in the appropriate caption or notes when necessary for accuracy; do not turn every spoken sentence into a verification checklist.

## Separate facts from expression

Maintain a claim table with evidence reference, time/version, public wording and remaining uncertainty. Creative wording may change rhythm or emphasis; it cannot create a new event.

Reject unsupported additions such as “I nearly gave up,” “it took ten minutes,” “my child loved it,” “everything works,” or “I have not touched it since.” These may sound natural but each adds a factual claim. Unknown camera or sleep behavior is not evidence that it failed. Earlier prototype testing does not verify a later design revision.

## Use an attention-grabbing opening the evidence can deliver

A surprising comparison can work if the actual object and context remain clear. Metaphors should not misidentify hardware, endorsement or measured capability. Show the claimed contrast promptly. Avoid promising outcomes absent from the footage.

For a fictional repair story, “This button used to do nothing. Watch it now.” can lead straight to a real before/after demonstration. “I fixed every problem forever” would require much broader evidence.

## Read it aloud and check coherence

- Would the author actually say this sentence?
- Does each technical detail help the viewer understand the result?
- Is there generic hype, an abstract moral or a call to action unrelated to the story?
- Does the ending deliver the opening's promise?
- Did the cover acquire a stronger claim than the script?
- Are filming instructions kept out of the spoken script?

Save the selected draft, not an unlabelled pile of alternatives. Keep raw helper responses privately for traceability, and distinguish the helper's text from subsequent human edits. A different model may improve phrasing; it does not remove the need for this review.
